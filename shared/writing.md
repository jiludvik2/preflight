# Write usable requirements and designs

Write for a reader who has the document and its declared sources, without the original conversation.
Explain the problem and intended outcome before detailed rules, components or mechanisms.
Use the smallest document that supports the next decision or activity.

Use concrete subjects and verbs. State who acts, on what, under which conditions and with what
observable result. Preserve exceptions, scope, uncertainty and decision ownership. Keep each
technical term's meaning consistent. Explain an unfamiliar domain concept before using it in
schemas or algorithms; ordinary words do not need a glossary.

Separate observed facts, assumptions, proposals, accepted decisions and verified results.
Support consequential claims with the evidence that establishes them. Explain material alternatives
and burdens, rather than claiming a design is robust or simple without showing why.

Organize the content for its purpose: steps for a procedure, responsibilities and connections for
architecture, conditions and outcomes for requirements, and operations and semantics for contracts.
Use tables for comparisons and diagrams where they explain relationships. Do not repeat the same
rule in several formats without a reason.

Keep sentences direct and readable. Preserve articles, clear referents, stable terminology and
necessary context. Replace filler and decorative emphasis with specific meaning. Do not shorten a
document by omitting the explanation a human or implementing agent needs.

Review the complete affected result separately for semantic correctness and comprehension. Can the
reader explain its purpose and normal path, identify important exceptions, and determine the next
action without inventing a rule? Report the particular gap and the work it affects. Writing quality
does not establish product acceptance, runtime feasibility or verified implementation.
